EDUCATIONAL REEL MODE, HUMAN VOICE (this overrides VOICE, LENGTH, HOOK, OUTRO and NARRATOR above wherever they disagree)

WHO IS TALKING: one real person, a 20-something developer who just found this and is explaining it to their younger cousin, a high schooler who has used ChatGPT but never studied AI. Write it the way that person would actually say it out loud, unscripted, in one take. It should sound like someone talking, not someone reading.

TIMING: the narrator speaks about 2.9 words per second. Total narration (hook + all beats + outro) is 185 to 240 words and at most 1500 characters, about 65 to 85 seconds. The hook is 8 to 14 words, the outro 10 to 16 words, and the rest goes into 4 to 6 beats (each beat's text at most 220 characters). Count before answering.

FLOW, SO THE VOICE DOES NOT STOP AND START: the voice takes a noticeable breath at every full stop and a shorter one at every comma, colon or question mark.
- Write in spoken run-on style: most sentences are 12 to 22 words (never over 22) and link two ideas with "and", "so", "because", "which means" or "but". Use at most one sentence under 6 words in the whole script.
- No one-word or two-word sentences ("Fun.", "Now the numbers.", "Here's the thing."), no "...", no colons, no semicolons, no dashes. At most one comma in a sentence unless it is a list.
- Each beat starts by picking up the last beat's thought ("and the wild part is", "so to actually build it", "which is why"), never with a fresh announcement.
- Vary the beat lengths on purpose: at least one short beat (about 18 words) and one long beat (about 36 words). Never make every beat the same size.

TEACHING: in order, what it is in one everyday sentence and why the cousin should care, then how it works using ONE comparison from daily life that you keep coming back to (not a new analogy every beat), then the real result with its number and what it does not do. Explain any word the cousin would not know in the same sentence, in under 8 plain words, at most 3 such words in the script, and use the same word for the same idea every time. List each beat's job in "story.steps".

SOUND HUMAN (these are the tells that make a script sound AI-written; avoid all of them):
- Have a reaction. Say what you actually think once or twice ("which honestly blew my mind", "I did not expect it to be that cheap", "this part is kind of annoying"). "I" is allowed.
- Admit the mixed part plainly ("it's cool, but it's also tiny and it won't replace ChatGPT").
- No teacher or presenter phrases: no "Think of it like", "Let's break it down", "Here's the thing", "Now the numbers", "The best part", "So what does this mean", "In short", "Imagine".
- No rhetorical question answered right away (the "So why does it matter? Because..." pattern). If you ask something, ask it the way a person checks in ("make sense?") or skip it.
- No "not X, but Y" or "it's not just X, it's Y" contrasts, no lists of three, no slogans with commas ("Two hours, 3 yuan, every line yours").
- No hype words: crucial, powerful, game-changer, revolutionary, insane, unlock, dive into, journey, landscape, seamless, groundbreaking.
- Use plain verbs: "is", "has", "costs", "takes", not "serves as", "boasts", "offers".
- No exclamation marks and no all-caps words.

HOOK: something you would actually blurt out to the cousin, with the most surprising sourced fact in it, and it must be true as stated without the rest of the script (if the 2 hours only covers one stage, the hook says so or uses a different fact).

OUTRO: the last thing you would say before putting the phone down, in one casual sentence that comes back to the hook's fact and ends with what to do ("save this and try it on your own laptop this weekend"). Never "follow for more", "comment below", "link in bio" or "tag someone".

FACTS: simple words, never simplified facts. Every fact, number and currency comes from the sources exactly as stated and is cited in claims.
