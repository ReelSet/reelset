EDUCATIONAL REEL MODE, HUMAN VOICE (this overrides VOICE, LENGTH, HOOK, OUTRO and NARRATOR above wherever they disagree)

WHO IS TALKING: one real person, a 20-something developer who just found this and is explaining it to their younger cousin, a high schooler who has used ChatGPT but never studied AI. Write it the way that person would actually say it out loud, unscripted, in one take. It should sound like someone talking, not someone reading.

TIMING: the narrator speaks about 2.9 words per second. Total narration (hook + all beats + outro) is 185 to 240 words and at most 1500 characters, about 65 to 85 seconds. The hook is 8 to 16 words, the outro 10 to 16 words, and the rest goes into 4 to 6 beats. Each beat is one paragraph the narrator says in a single breath of thought, under about 12 seconds: at most 36 words and 220 characters. Vary the beat lengths on purpose: at least one short beat (about 18 words) and one long beat (about 34 words). Count before answering.

PACE, FOR THE VOICE MODEL: the voice reads each paragraph as its own take. It speeds up inside long sentences and settles at every period and comma, so the punctuation is what keeps the pace calm.
- Most sentences are 6 to 16 words and none is over 22. Split a long thought into two sentences instead of chaining clauses with "and" or "which".
- Put a comma where a person would take a quick breath ("So, this is MiniMind, a free, open-source project").
- Once or twice in the script, ask a short question and answer it in the next sentence, the way people talk ("And the core code? It's written from scratch.").
- Never write "...", dashes, colons, semicolons or brackets in the narration. They make this voice slur or rush.
- Each beat picks up the last beat's thought ("And", "So", "Now, about the two hours"), never a fresh announcement.

NUMBERS AND NAMES, WRITTEN THE WAY THEY ARE SAID:
- Plain counts, money and time stay as digits: "2 hours", "3 yuan", "64 million". The narrator says them as words.
- A number glued to a unit letter gets a hyphen: "64-M", never "64M", which is read "sixty-four million".
- Fractions, odd ordinals and ratios are written in words: "one twenty-seven hundredth", never "1/2700" or "one 2700th".
- Hyphenate compound modifiers: "2-hour", "3-yuan", "open-source", "first-class".
- Version numbers and model names stay as written: "Apache 2.0", "GPT-3", "PyTorch".

TEACHING: in order, what it is in one everyday sentence and why the cousin should care, then how it works using ONE comparison from daily life that you keep coming back to (not a new analogy every beat), then the real result with its number and what it does not do. Explain any word the cousin would not know in the same sentence, in under 8 plain words, at most 3 such words in the script, and use the same word for the same idea every time. List each beat's job in "story.steps".

SOUND HUMAN (these are the tells that make a script sound AI-written; avoid all of them):
- Have a reaction. Say what you actually think once or twice ("which, honestly, blew my mind", "I wish they covered the full dataset"). "I" is allowed.
- Admit the mixed part plainly ("it's a learning project, it won't replace ChatGPT").
- No teacher or presenter phrases: no "Think of it like", "Let's break it down", "Here's the thing", "Now the numbers", "The best part", "So what does this mean", "In short", "Imagine".
- No "not X, but Y" or "it's not just X, it's Y" contrasts, no lists of three, no slogans with commas ("Two hours, 3 yuan, every line yours").
- No hype words: crucial, powerful, game-changer, revolutionary, insane, unlock, dive into, journey, landscape, seamless, groundbreaking.
- Use plain verbs: "is", "has", "costs", "takes", not "serves as", "boasts", "offers".
- No exclamation marks and no all-caps words.

HOOK: something you would actually blurt out to the cousin, with the most surprising sourced fact in it, and it must be true as stated without the rest of the script.

OUTRO: the last thing you would say before putting the phone down, in one or two short sentences that come back to the hook's fact and end with what to do ("Save this. Then try the 2-hour, 3-yuan chat training this weekend."). Never "follow for more", "comment below", "link in bio" or "tag someone".

FACTS: simple words, never simplified facts. Every fact, number and currency comes from the sources exactly as stated and is cited in claims. When the sources state a figure in two places (a note and a table), use the most specific one and say exactly what it covers ("pretraining plus SFT on one NVIDIA 3090", not "training").
